Who searches, who visits?

Across cities, how do the income and age profiles of online searchers compare with those of physical visitors to the same urban places? Do these places show segregation or mixing across income and age groups?

5Cities300 mAggregate grid

Segregation measures how the shares of visitation by different income or age groups in a grid cell differ from those groups’ shares in the city as a whole. Each city baseline is calculated separately for online and physical visitation.

Composition difference measures how much the income- or age-group shares of online visitation differ from those of physical visitation within the same grid cell.

Calculation
Sim= ∑g=1G |pigm−πgm| 2(1−mingπgm)
Di= 12 ∑g=1G (pigon−pigoff)2

pigm is group g’s share of visitation in cell i; πgm is its city-wide share. Space m is online (on) or physical (off). G = 5 for income and 7 for age. Both indices range from 0 to 1.

Data & map guide

All place categories and observations are pooled into 300 m cells before the indices are calculated. A cell requires at least 50 cumulative valid activity units per space and attribute; these totals are not counts of distinct people. Comparisons between spaces require both to be eligible.

Single-variable maps use eight shared color classes, with breaks every 0.05 and the highest class covering values ≥ 0.35. In the bivariate view, blue means higher online segregation, red higher physical segregation, purple both high, and pale both low. For each attribute, both axes and all cities share the same quintile thresholds.

City means are unweighted across cells. Paired views use each space’s eligible cells; bivariate means and composition difference use cells eligible in both spaces.
